# AI disclosure notes

This is orientation, not legal advice. Rules differ by country, platform and sponsor, and they change. Check the platform's current policy for anything that matters. The facts below were checked against official sources on the dates in `platform-specs.md`.

## What the platforms say

| Platform | Position |
|---|---|
| YouTube | Creators must disclose realistic altered or synthetic content when they upload (required since 2024-03-18). Text drafted with AI is not covered by that rule. |
| TikTok | Realistic AI-generated content must be labelled. TikTok says labelled content stays eligible for the For You feed. |
| LinkedIn | LinkedIn says it does not remove AI-assisted posts and that posts with real perspective are fine. It has said it reduces the reach of generic AI posts and comments that lack a point of view. |
| Instagram, Threads | Meta has labelling policies for realistic AI-generated images, video and audio. Check current Meta policy for what applies to text. |
| X, Bluesky | No general rule for AI-assisted text found at the time of the last review. Check current terms for automated posting. |

Disclosure rules mostly cover generated media, not text a person reviewed and approved. The practical risk with text is generic, unsourced writing, which is what the claims check and the specificity pass target.

## Other rules that may apply

- Paid or sponsored content has its own disclosure rules in many countries (for example the US FTC endorsement guides). They apply whether or not AI wrote the draft.
- Some employers, publishers and clients have their own AI policies. Ask if the user works for one.
- Some regions have AI transparency laws that take effect on fixed dates. If the user publishes to those audiences, they should check the current text of the law.

## How to use this in a job

- Do not add a disclosure line to the content unless the user asks for one or `disclosure_default` is `on`.
- If media is involved (video scripts, images described in a carousel), mention once at hand-off that the platform may require a label when realistic synthetic media is used.
- If the user asks for "no one will know it is AI", decline that framing. Offer the specificity edit and a clean review instead.
- If the user wants a disclosure line, offer a plain one the person can edit, such as "Drafted with AI assistance and edited by me." Never invent a person's agreement to a policy.

## EU AI Act Article 50

Transparency duties under Article 50 have applied since 2 August 2026, with the marking duty for already-marketed generative systems deferred to 2 December 2026. Obligations differ for the tool provider and for the person who publishes. The Commission's guidelines were still a draft when this was written, so treat the points below as cautions and check current guidance.

- Published AI-generated text on matters of public interest may need a disclosure unless a person has reviewed it and holds editorial responsibility.
- The review exemption is expected to require real review and fact-checking. This is why content-wand finishes all edits, including the humanizer pass, before the review packet, and why an edit after approval needs a new approval.
- Never fabricate testimonials, reviews or first-person experience. The US FTC rule on consumer reviews and testimonials (effective 2024-10-21) applies whether or not AI wrote the text.
